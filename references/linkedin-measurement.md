# LinkedIn measurement framework

Use this reference for post reviews, content experiments, and planning decisions based on supplied or authorized analytics.

## Start with the decision

Name the review period, comparison period, included accounts, content set, business objective, and decision the analysis should support. Record metric definitions from the source because exports and tools can label or calculate metrics differently.

## Evidence levels

- **Observed:** a value or pattern directly present in the supplied data.
- **Calculated:** a reproducible transformation; state the formula and denominator.
- **Hypothesis:** a possible explanation that needs testing.
- **Recommendation:** an action connected to an observation and a measurable next check.

Never turn a correlation into a cause, generalize from a tiny sample without a caveat, or infer business impact from platform engagement alone.

## Useful comparisons

- Compare like with like: same metric definition, compatible time windows, account type, and content scope.
- Use rates when exposure differs, but retain the underlying counts and state the denominator.
- Examine medians or distributions when a few outliers distort averages.
- Tag content by meaningful variables such as audience, objective, theme, evidence type, format, author, and call to action.
- Mark launches, paid support, major news, missing values, and deleted content that may affect interpretation.

## Review output

Provide:

1. coverage and caveats;
2. a performance snapshot tied to the objective;
3. observed patterns with supporting rows or examples;
4. hypotheses clearly separated from findings;
5. one to three tests with variable, control, success measure, and review window;
6. instrumentation or data gaps.

Do not promise reach, engagement, leads, or growth. Do not manufacture benchmarks when no relevant benchmark was supplied.
