# LinkedIn hook patterns

Use this reference when generating or testing opening lines. A hook is a framing decision, not a detached slogan; judge it with the post body and the source evidence.

## Choose an angle

| Angle | Use when | Opening move | Common failure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Observation | The author has noticed a defensible pattern | Name the pattern and its setting | Presenting one anecdote as a trend |
| Tension | Two priorities genuinely conflict | Put the tradeoff in plain language | Inventing controversy |
| Outcome | A result is sourced and attributable | Lead with the result, then qualify it | Hiding timeframe or conditions |
| Contrarian | Evidence challenges a familiar assumption | State the assumption and the evidence | Disagreeing only for attention |
| Practical | The reader has a specific job to do | Promise a concrete takeaway | Vague numbered-list bait |
| Story | A real moment creates the insight | Begin near the decision or change | Fabricating dialogue or drama |
| Question | The audience can answer from experience | Ask one precise question | Asking a broad engagement prompt |

## Variant protocol

Create variants only across a named dimension: angle, specificity, tone, or audience awareness. Keep the body promise stable so the comparison is meaningful. Label what changed and why it may fit the objective; do not predict which variant will “go viral.”

Score each hook from 1 to 3 on:

- source fidelity;
- audience relevance;
- specificity;
- body alignment;
- voice fit.

Reject any hook that fails source fidelity or promises more than the post delivers, regardless of its total score.
