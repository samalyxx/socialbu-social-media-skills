# LinkedIn content planning framework

Use this reference for editorial calendars, content pillars, repurposing queues, advocacy programs, and story capture.

## Planning inputs

Anchor a plan in a defined date range, audience, business objective, reader value, author or page, capacity, source material, campaign dates, review constraints, and evidence from prior content. Treat missing capacity and cadence as explicit assumptions.

## Build the plan

1. Translate the business objective into an observable reader action or learning outcome.
2. Choose three to five working themes that connect audience problems, credible expertise, and the objective.
3. Give each proposed item a purpose: teach, demonstrate, explain a point of view, share proof, invite a substantive conversation, or support a campaign.
4. Vary the evidence and treatment, not merely the surface format.
5. Assign a source, owner, status, approval path, and production dependency.
6. Identify gaps instead of filling calendar slots with weak ideas.

## Editorial record

Use these fields where relevant:

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Date or window | Proposed timing, with timezone when exact |
| Author/account | Personal profile or organization page; never assume access |
| Audience | The specific reader segment |
| Objective | The one intended outcome |
| Theme and angle | Subject plus the distinct treatment |
| Format | Text, document, image, video, poll, or other requested format |
| Source | Link, note, interview, data, or approved claim supporting the item |
| CTA | Optional next action |
| Owner | Person responsible for the next step |
| Status | Idea, briefed, drafting, review, approved, scheduled, or published |

Do not claim that a cadence, format, or time is optimal without account-specific evidence. A content plan is not authorization to schedule it.

## Repurposing test

A derivative should preserve the source meaning while creating a new reader experience. Record the source insight, facts and attribution that must survive, the destination audience, the new angle, and what was deliberately omitted. Do not turn one source into many near-duplicates solely to fill a quota.
