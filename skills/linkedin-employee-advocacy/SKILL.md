# LinkedIn employee advocacy

Create a program people can join voluntarily and adapt truthfully. Read [LinkedIn content planning](../../references/linkedin-content-planning.md), [LinkedIn writing](../../references/linkedin-writing.md), and [LinkedIn conversations](../../references/linkedin-conversations.md).

## Intake

Get the campaign objective, approved source material, eligible participants, relevant expertise, regions or disclosure rules supplied by the organization, voice constraints, participation window, review process, prohibited claims, and support owner. Ask whether participants have genuine experience with the subject before proposing first-person copy.

## Workflow

1. Extract approved facts, links, media, branded terms, and required disclosures.
2. Define a small set of participation routes: original post, personal perspective, expert explanation, thoughtful comment, or optional reshare context.
3. Match routes to participant roles and actual experience; do not assign personal claims.
4. Build an advocacy brief with purpose, facts, boundaries, examples, and a clear opt-out.
5. Draft starting points that require personalization rather than identical finished posts for mass distribution.
6. Add a review path for regulated, customer, performance, employment, or confidential claims.
7. Define measurement at an aggregate, program-relevant level without ranking or pressuring individuals.

## Output

Provide `Campaign brief`, `Approved fact pack`, `Participation options`, `Personalization prompts`, `Sample starting points`, `Review and escalation`, and `Measurement`. Label each sample by the experience it assumes and remove it if that experience is not verified.

Never fabricate enthusiasm, employment experience, product use, customer knowledge, or results. Do not encourage coordinated deceptive engagement, require personal-account participation, or imply that a plan has been distributed or published.
