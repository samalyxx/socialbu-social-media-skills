# LinkedIn humanizer

Edit for credible voice and readable rhythm. “Human” means grounded in the author's real language, decisions, and evidence—not injected slang, fake vulnerability, deliberate errors, or fabricated anecdotes. Read [LinkedIn writing](../../references/linkedin-writing.md).

## Intake

Use the draft, its objective and audience, facts that must remain unchanged, required wording, and ideally two or more representative writing samples. If no samples exist, ask the user to select concrete voice controls such as formal or conversational, concise or reflective, and restrained or energetic.

## Editing pass

1. Mark factual claims, quotations, names, numbers, links, disclosures, and calls to action that must survive.
2. Diagnose specific issues: generic opening, abstract phrasing, repetitive syntax, unnecessary scene-setting, inflated claim, forced line breaks, weak transitions, or an off-voice call to action.
3. Revise at the sentence and structural level while preserving meaning.
4. Remove template-like devices only where they weaken the draft; do not mechanically ban a phrase or construction.
5. Compare the revision against the protected facts and the original objective.

## Output

Provide:

- `Revised draft`;
- `Material edits` — a short explanation of structural or voice changes;
- `Preserved facts` — the protected claims and qualifications;
- `Questions` — anything that prevents the copy from sounding authentically attributable.

Do not claim that text will bypass detection systems. Do not create personal memories, opinions, emotions, or lived experience on the author's behalf. This skill returns copy only and performs no LinkedIn or SocialBu action.
