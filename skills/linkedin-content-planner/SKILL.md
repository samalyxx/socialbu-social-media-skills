# LinkedIn content planner

Turn objectives and credible source material into a plan a team can produce. Read [LinkedIn content planning](../../references/linkedin-content-planning.md); use [LinkedIn measurement](../../references/linkedin-measurement.md) when prior performance data is supplied.

## Intake

Establish the planning period, profile or page, audience segments, business objective, reader value, campaigns and fixed dates, source inventory, production capacity, author availability, approval path, prior evidence, and measurement approach.

When capacity or cadence is missing, propose a conservative working assumption and make it visible. Do not fill every date if the sources cannot support distinct, useful posts.

## Workflow

1. Convert the objective into one or more observable content outcomes.
2. Define three to five working themes with a clear audience problem and evidence base.
3. Audit available stories, expertise, data, product updates, customer-approved proof, and existing assets.
4. Map ideas to themes and objectives; remove duplicates and unsupported concepts.
5. Sequence posts around dependencies and campaign dates while leaving room for timely material.
6. Assign owner, source, production needs, approval status, and measurement intent to every item.
7. Identify evidence, capacity, or coverage gaps.

## Output

Return:

- `Strategy` — audience, objective, themes, and working assumptions;
- `Editorial plan` — a table using the fields in the planning reference;
- `Briefs` — one paragraph per item covering angle, evidence, and reader value;
- `Production queue` — ordered next actions, owners, and dependencies;
- `Measurement plan` — a small set of objective-linked checks;
- `Gaps and decisions` — unresolved inputs requiring a human.

This skill proposes timing but does not schedule content. Do not describe a posting time or cadence as optimal without account-specific evidence.
