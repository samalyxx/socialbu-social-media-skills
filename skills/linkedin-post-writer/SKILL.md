# LinkedIn post writer

Create publish-ready LinkedIn copy without inventing expertise, results, or personal experience. Read [LinkedIn writing](../../references/linkedin-writing.md); read [LinkedIn hooks](../../references/linkedin-hooks.md) when the opening needs variants or testing.

## Intake

Establish:

- author or organization and intended reader;
- one objective and one desired reader response;
- source facts, proof, examples, and required attribution;
- voice samples or concrete voice constraints;
- format, call to action, link, mention, disclosure, and asset requirements;
- claims or topics that require review.

If an essential fact is absent, ask for it. If the missing detail is nonessential, make a conservative choice and list it under `Assumptions` rather than inserting an editorial marker into the copy.

## Workflow

1. Write a one-sentence content brief: audience, problem or interest, core point, proof, and objective.
2. Choose a structure suited to that material: insight and evidence, problem and method, story and lesson, point of view, case note, or concise announcement.
3. Draft an opening whose promise the source can support, then make the body fulfill it.
4. Use only the supplied evidence. Preserve qualifications, attribution, branded terms, and required language.
5. End with the natural implication or a relevant next action. Do not append a generic question for engagement.
6. Run the quality gate in the writing reference.

## Output

Return:

- `Brief` — the audience, objective, and central idea in one or two sentences;
- `Draft` — clean copy ready for human review;
- `Editorial notes` — intentional structure, formatting, link, media, or mention decisions;
- `Facts to verify` — only claims that still need confirmation;
- `Assumptions` — any non-factual choices made.

Offer alternate openings only when requested or when they represent a defined test. This skill does not publish or schedule. If the user requests execution, hand the final approved copy to `socialbu-linkedin-publisher`.
